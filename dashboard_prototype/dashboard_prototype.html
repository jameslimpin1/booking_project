Booking value doesn't predict who cancels — but it predicts what it costs us

We split bookings into five equal-sized groups by price. The cancellation rate is flat across every group (14–15%) — a $2,500 booking is no more likely to cancel than a $250 one. But because higher-value bookings lose more revenue when they do cancel, the top price fifth accounts for 43% of all revenue lost to cancellation ($5.6M of $12.8M), despite being just 20% of bookings.

Prioritize the unanswered-opening queue by booking value

The 86 bookings with a completely unanswered opening message (Section 3) are cheap to clear entirely — but if support capacity is ever constrained, work that list highest-value first. It won't change the odds of any one booking cancelling, but it protects more revenue per reply sent.

✅

Recommended next step

Add a response-time alert for any guest message left unanswered beyond a set window (e.g. a few hours). This directly targets the only pattern in the data clearly linked to lost bookings, is cheap to implement, and doesn't require interpreting message content or sentiment.
